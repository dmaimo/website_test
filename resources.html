Data

A list of data resources I have found useful in empirical analysis.

V-Dem Project

More than 600 indicators of democracy for 200+ countries, from 1789 to today. Co-hosted by the University of Gothenburg and the Kellogg Institute.

Quality of Government

Quality of democracy, corruption, bureaucracy and institutional indicators, University of Gothenburg.

Macrohistory Database

Jordà–Schularick–Taylor: 18 advanced economies, 48 real and nominal variables, annually since 1870 (release R.6).

Barro–Lee Dataset

Educational attainment and human capital for 146 countries, 1950–2015 (dataset last updated September 2021).

World Income Inequality Database

Income inequality for 200+ countries, 1867 to today.

World Inequality Database

Income distribution, wealth and top incomes for 100+ countries, 1820 to today. Stata: ssc install wid.

Fraser Institute — Economic Freedom

Economic freedom index for 165 countries and territories, from 1970 onwards.

Heritage Foundation — Economic Freedom

Annual economic freedom index for 180+ countries, 1995 onwards.

KOF Globalisation Index

Economic, social and political globalisation for 190+ countries, 1970 onwards.

Eurostat

European official statistics.

VOLARE — VOLatility Archive for Realized Estimates

An open research infrastructure (hosted at the University of Messina) providing realized volatility and covariance measures from ultra-high-frequency data on stocks, exchange rates and futures, including interactive visualization and real-time HAR/MEM model estimation.

Itiner-e — Digital Atlas of Ancient Roads

An open, GIS-ready dataset of the Roman road network, with each segment geo-referenced (GeoJSON/ndjson) plus a route-finding tool for ancient travel times. Useful for spatial analysis in a historical context linking geography, market access and long-run regional development.

Human Mobility Index

GIS raster data (GeoTiff, 1km/10km resolution) estimating minimum travel time across the globe before the widespread use of steam power, accounting for terrain, biological constraints and (optionally) seafaring. Used to construct historical isolation, trade-network and market-access measures; a companion Python package (hmi) computes travel-time distances directly.

Global Macro Database

A harmonized panel of 46 macro-financial variables for 239 countries and territories, 1086 to today, built from over 150 contemporary and historical sources (IMF, World Bank, OECD, plus long-run country archives).

World Bank Business Ready

The World Bank's successor to Doing Business (discontinued in 2021 over data-integrity concerns), redesigned with independent third-party data collection. Benchmarks regulatory framework, public services and operational efficiency for the private sector; 101 economies in the 2025 report, expanding toward ~180 by 2027.

Stata

Stata is my software of choice for empirical work: user-friendly for beginners, fully programmable for advanced users, and backed by thousands of freely downloadable community routines. Alternatives worth knowing are Matlab (very powerful), EViews (popular in finance, but static) and R (open source and flexible).

Routines I find particularly useful

Most user-written routines below can be installed from the command window with: ssc install <name>.

xtabond2
A largely-used routine for dynamic panel data models with the Arellano–Bond or Blundell–Bond estimators: more flexible and informative than the official routines. Roodman (2009), Stata Journal 9(1), pp. 86–136.
xtlsdvc
Bias-corrected LSDV estimators for standard dynamic panel data models. Bruno (2005), Stata Journal 5(4), pp. 473–500.
xtdpdbc
Bias-corrected method-of-moments estimator for linear dynamic panel data models (Breitung, Kripfganz and Hayakawa, 2021); corrects the Nickell bias of the fixed-effects estimator without relying on instruments, typically retaining FE's smaller variance relative to GMM.
xtdpdgmm
GMM estimators for linear dynamic panel data models; reproduces xtabond2 results and adds the Ahn–Schmidt (1995) nonlinear moment conditions, useful under high persistence.
getsymbols & mvport
Download financial time series (Yahoo Finance, Alpha Vantage, Quandl…) and compute minimum-variance portfolios.
kountry
A 'universal' converter that standardises country names and codes across sources, making it much easier to merge macro datasets.
utest
Correct test for a U-shaped (or inverse U-shaped) relationship on an interval, implementing Lind and Mehlum (2010).
inteff
Correct marginal effects — and standard errors — of interacted variables in logit and probit models.
nwcommands
A full suite for network analysis in Stata; see the companion website for installation.
ivreg2
IV estimation with much richer diagnostics on instrument validity than the official ivregress.
ivreg2h
Lewbel (2012): IV estimation with instruments generated from heteroskedastic errors, when external instruments are unavailable or weak.
grstyle
Set the overall look of graphs from within a do-file — palettes, absolute sizes — without touching the graph itself.
esttab
The reference routine to export Stata results to LaTeX; the estout companion site documents the whole family.

Just for fun

Animated graphs

Stata can produce animated GIFs by generating multiple frames and assembling them with FFMPEG — I use it to visualise spillover effects in a spatial autoregressive (SAR) model of homicide and unemployment across Texas counties. Robert Grant's page on animated graphs is a useful reference.

Pairs trading strategy

Pairs trading is a market-neutral strategy that exploits temporary divergences between two historically co-moving assets: when the spread between their prices widens beyond its normal range, you short the outperforming asset and go long the underperforming one, betting on reversion to their historical relationship. This package contains the Stata files to identify candidate pairs and backtest the strategy. A Python script that replicates the same analysis is available here.

LaTeX

LaTeX is a high-quality typesetting system and the de facto standard for scientific writing. I moved from Word to Scientific WorkPlace and finally to pure LaTeX, and I have not regretted the choice. My favourite setup is TeXworks with MiKTeX: auto-completion, unicode support and a nice built-in PDF viewer. Beyond papers, LaTeX (with the TikZ package) can draw diagrams, game trees and best-response functions, and Beamer can animate them in presentations.
